03 + 04 / EXECUTION & TIMING

Twelve weeks to decide. Six weeks to prepare.

Proposed seasonal window: late April–May 2027, subject to readiness. The supplied German demand index climbs from 98 in April to 118 in May and 138 in July. It is a historical case pattern, not a Berlin 2027 forecast.

Learn before the seasonal peak

A week-12 review cannot mature every customer cohort.

Use fixed 30- and 60-day windows from each customer’s first paid order. Keep late cohorts labelled “not mature”; never count them as non-repeaters.

Measurement contract and weekly operating rhythm

Sampling: count unique, consented participants separately from cans handed out. Match a participant token or QR attribution to a first paid purchase within 14 days; report unmatched purchases and attribution limitations separately.

Repeat: distinct first-time buyers with at least one subsequent paid order in 30/60 days ÷ distinct first-time buyers with the full corresponding follow-up window. Exclude cancelled and fully refunded orders; do not count free replacements or subscriptions without a second paid order.

Acquisition economics: allocate sampling, labor, venue, creative and paid-media costs to acquired first-time buyers by source. Compare cumulative contribution after variable costs and refunds with acquisition cost for the same mature cohort. Treat a zero-buyer source as unrecovered spend, not zero CAC.

Weekly cadence: Growth reviews paid conversion; CRM reviews mature cohorts; Operations reviews units per active outlet-week, stockouts and replenishment; Finance reconciles costs and cash; the CEO decides at weeks 2, 6 and 12.

Data: record dates, anonymous buyer ID, source, paid units, net revenue, variable costs, refunds and partner ID. Collect consent where required, limit access and retention, and keep personal details out of the CEO dashboard.

Recovery is contribution-based, not cash payback. Timing of inventory payments, working capital, taxes, deposits and receivables requires a separate cash plan. No validated Berlin return is shown.

Formula, cost boundaries and downside cases

Weighted contribution = Σ(paid-unit channel share × case contribution) − DTC share × extra DTC cost. Recovery spending = non-product launch spend + sample cans × €0.62. Recovery units = round up(spending ÷ positive weighted contribution). If contribution is zero or negative, volume cannot recover the spending.

At €2.19 and 45/40/15: 0.45 × €1.16 + 0.40 × €0.63 + 0.15 × €1.13 = €0.9435. €10,000 ÷ €0.9435 requires 10,599 paid cans. At €0.30 extra DTC cost, contribution falls to €0.8085 and recovery needs 12,369 cans. At 20% lower contribution, the same €10,000 needs 13,249 cans. These are sensitivities, not probabilities.

Case contributions already subtract €0.62 COGS per paid can and stated channel allowances. Do not subtract them twice. Confirm extra partner fees, wastage, returns, discounts, storage and delivery through supplier quotes; only then approve the budget. Sample COGS is separate because samples produce no paid revenue.

Expand carefully

Only when mature demand and contribution meet agreed gates, data is reliable, and fulfillment capacity is confirmed.

Extend or adjust

If evidence is too immature or too small, continue a capped follow-up. If one channel fails, isolate and retest the specific cause.

Pause investment

Stop acquisition at the cash cap or if corrected unit contribution is non-positive. Resolve product, cost or tracking failures before restarting.
