# LUMEN Berlin pilot — CEO decision memo

Prepared 16 September 2026. Proposed launch: 3 May 2027. Status: planning recommendation; no pilot results, approved budget or confirmed partners supplied.

## Decision requested

Approve preparation for a focused 12-week Berlin pilot, targeting Urban Wellness Professionals and an afternoon-coffee alternative occasion. Release launch funds only after named evidence owners verify partner access, costs, inventory, tracking and decision thresholds. Preserve the option to choose another city if its executable operating plan is stronger.

The commercial hypothesis is €2.19 per can, physical tasting for discovery, and online repeat purchasing. Proposed paid-unit mix: 45% DTC, 40% selected retail, 15% Gym & Office. This is neither a sales forecast nor an acquisition-budget split.

The resulting city launch indicator is categorical: **conditional pilot**, rather than a fabricated weighted score. Audience evidence is available; commercial execution and repeat economics are unproven. A high intent score must not compensate for an unworkable cost structure.

## 2. Price, route to market and coverage

- Start at €2.19. National case acceptance is 51.7% at that price versus 26.7% at €2.59; these are not Berlin conversion rates.
- DTC 45%: repeat orders, customer feedback and identifiable purchase cohorts. Compare 6- and 12-can packs without assuming either will be accepted.
- Retail 40%: selective discovery and convenient paid trial; capture sell-through and stockouts, not just shipments into stores.
- Gym & Office 15%: controlled occasion and secondary-audience testing. Separate paid units from free partner samples.
- Begin with 6–10 qualified partners in two compact clusters. Eight partners, split six retail and two Gym & Office, is an operating illustration, not a signed distribution plan.

A six-pack is €13.14 and a 12-pack €26.28 at the headline price. Tax, deposit, delivery and discounts must be reconciled before these become final checkout totals. The 12-pack exceeds the Berlin wellness sample's €21.90 mean reported monthly beverage spend; test purchase commitment and pack economics.

Partner selection template: candidate name/address; audience access; tasting permission; opening terms; expected active weeks; opening stock; sales-reporting agreement; route cost; QR/source code; replenishment owner; contract status. Do not record hypothetical venues as confirmed.

## 3. Execution, measurement and review

| Stage | Dates for 3 May start | Deliverables | Owner / decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Six-week preparation | 22 Mar–2 May 2027 | Partner terms, actual unit costs, stock, checkout, attribution test, approved cash cap and gates | Commercial, Operations, Finance / ready to launch? |
| Weeks 1–2 | 3–16 May | Controlled sampling, paid trial, taste and price feedback, source tracking | Growth / is paid trial occurring? |
| Weeks 3–6 | 17 May–13 Jun | Source economics, early repeats, outlet sales, stockouts and partner reorders | Growth, CRM, Commercial / where to reallocate? |
| Weeks 7–12 | 14 Jun–25 Jul | Mature cohort repeats, actual customer contribution, operating capacity and cash | CEO + Finance / expand, adjust or pause? |

Paid conversion: first paid buyers attributable within 14 days / unique tracked sampled people with a complete observation window. Number of free cans may differ from number of people. Report source coverage and unmatched buyers; last-click QR attribution is not causal incrementality.

Repeat rate: buyers with at least one subsequent paid order within 30/60 days of first paid purchase / first-time buyers with a complete 30/60-day window. Remove cancelled and fully refunded orders; do not count free replacements. Separate the two cumulative windows and report numerator, denominator and cohort dates.

At the 25 July review, a 60-day window is complete only for first buyers acquired on or before **26 May**, and a 30-day window only for those acquired on or before **25 June**. A buyer first acquired on 25 July needs follow-up to **23 September** for a 60-day result. Later cohorts remain “not mature.” Reserve follow-up staffing and cost beyond week 12.

Source CAC: allocated acquisition spend / first-time paid buyers for the same source and period. Include sampling product costs, labor and partner/creative/media costs under an agreed allocation; exclude repeat buyers from the new-customer denominator. If there are no acquired buyers, display unrecovered spend rather than zero CAC. Avoid double-counting the same sampling expense in pilot profitability and cohort recovery.

Cohort contribution: net paid revenue less actual variable product, channel, order-handling, delivery, discount, refund and loss costs. Compare accumulated contribution with acquisition cost for the same eligible cohort; do not multiply modeled lifetime value by survey intent.

Weekly reporting: total and source-level samplers, paid first buyers, units, net revenue, mature 30/60-day repeats, contribution, cash used, active outlet-weeks, stockouts and partner reorders. The CEO view contains aggregates, not survey names or emails.

## 4. Timing and readiness

Late April or May 2027 is a proposed learning window. The case national index is 98 in April, 118 in May, 132 in June and 138 in July. Higher demand may assist trial but does not correct poor execution. An autumn test should be smaller and interpreted as off-peak evidence.

The inherited Timing lab remains a national seasonal heuristic. It is not a city ranking, a probability of success, or proof of readiness. One year's tracked promotions does not establish a clear future shelf. Refresh real competitor and partner conditions before spending.

Five documentary launch gates: (1) qualified partners and feasible coverage; (2) quotes, checkout treatment, cash cap and runway; (3) available quality-approved stock and replenishment; (4) working purchase attribution and cohort tracking; (5) CEO-approved owners, thresholds, reviews and stop rules. Dashboard checkboxes are resettable meeting notes, not authorization or verification.

## 5. Contribution recovery and expansion

At €2.19, case unit contribution is €1.16 DTC, €0.63 retail and €1.13 Gym & Office (`data/price_test_results.csv`). For 45/40/15:

`0.45 × 1.16 + 0.40 × 0.63 + 0.15 × 1.13 = €0.9435 per paid can`.

Editable spending illustration: €8,760 non-product launch spending plus 2,000 sample cans × €0.62 = €10,000 recovery spending. The €8,760 is an unapproved placeholder for labor, partner costs, creative/media, setup, follow-up and contingency; obtain a costed allocation. Case paid-can contribution already includes COGS; subtract sample COGS separately and do not subtract paid COGS again.

| Sensitivity | Contribution / paid can | Paid cans to recover €10,000 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Case 45/40/15 mix | €0.9435 | 10,599 |
| Additional €0.30 per DTC paid can | €0.8085 | 12,369 |
| 20% lower blended contribution | €0.7548 | 13,249 |

Case DTC economics already contain €0.35 fulfillment allowance per paid can plus payment processing. The dashboard extra-cost input means incremental cost beyond those allowances. At zero, that incremental amount is unverified; it is not a claim of free shipping. Quoted refunds, losses and discounts also need reconciliation.

The default 12,000 paid-can volume is a planning input, not a forecast. At the case mix it produces €11,322 contribution and €1,322 after the €10,000 hurdle, before unmodeled costs. It implies 450 equivalent DTC 12-pack orders, 66.7 retail cans per outlet-week across six stores, and 75 Gym & Office cans per partner-week across two partners, assuming 12 active weeks. Orders are not unique customers; fulfillment capacity and these sales velocities require validation.

Recovery units = ceiling(recovery spending / positive blended contribution). At non-positive contribution, extra volume cannot recover spending. This model does not calculate a cash-payback date or fund opening inventory; Finance needs a weekly cash calendar with payment and receivable timing.

### Proposed decision rules — to agree before launch

These are discussion assumptions, not industry benchmarks or case evidence.

| Metric | Proposed threshold | Evidence minimum / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling-to-paid trial | ≥10% within 14 days | ≥200 fully observed tracked unique participants; report matching coverage |
| 30-day repeat | ≥20% | ≥100 eligible DTC first buyers; report count and uncertainty |
| 60-day repeat | ≥30% | ≥100 eligible DTC first buyers; do not include immature cohorts |
| DTC acquisition recovery | 60-day accumulated contribution / acquisition cost ≥1.0 | Same mature source/cohort and complete cost allocation |
| Partner reorder | ≥60% within 30 days | Only partners with a full window; show absolute counts |
| Financial discipline | Positive actual contribution and cash within cap | Reconcile all incremental costs, stock funding and capacity |

The sample minima are operational checkpoints, not a statistical power calculation. Before launch, choose a meaningful effect size and confidence policy; a small observed percentage alone should not trigger expansion. A 60-day acquisition-recovery requirement is demanding and may need revision before launch once actual costs and expected order size are known. Do not relax it retroactively to declare success.

Expand a limited next wave only when demand, contribution, capacity and agreed evidence rules support it. If cohorts are immature or undersized, extend follow-up under a capped budget. If a source is uneconomic, isolate the cause and retest. Pause new acquisition on cash-cap breach, non-positive actual contribution, product quality failures or unusable tracking.
